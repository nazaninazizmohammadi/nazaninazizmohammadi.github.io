<p style="font-size: 1.12rem; font-weight: 600;">Reflections on State Responsibility for Protecting Women from Domestic Violence</p>

**Nazanin Azizmohammadi, Ph.D.**

<p class="page__meta">November 25, 2026 · English summary of a Persian essay</p>

The state already shapes power within the family. It determines spouses’ rights and responsibilities and the decisions that receive legal backing. In Iran, rules governing family leadership, residence, employment and married women’s passports make this role visible. Treating domestic violence as a purely private matter cannot be separated from the state’s own role in structuring family relationships.

In this essay, I examine that tension through the feminist critique of the public–private divide, drawing on Carole Pateman and Susan Moller Okin. Protecting family privacy should preserve intimacy and personal autonomy. It should not prevent scrutiny of unequal power or obstruct a woman’s access to safety and justice.

Iranian law contains protective tools, including separate residence where a wife faces a risk of harm and divorce on grounds of intolerable hardship. Social emergency services and safe houses also provide support. Their existence, however, does not by itself establish a coordinated, accessible system of protection. A legal right to complain may offer little practical security when a woman lacks a safe place to stay or the means to support herself.

The essay connects these gaps to the long legislative process surrounding protection from violence and to Iran’s binding obligations under the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights. It distinguishes those obligations from specific policy proposals. The proposed approach links prevention, urgent protection and support after harm, including shelter, healthcare, legal assistance and economic support.

Protective intervention must preserve women’s ability to make decisions about their own lives. The test is whether a woman can seek safety without being forced to sacrifice her autonomy or economic survival.
