This paper examines the recent proposal to amend the rules governing *mahr* within the broader structure of Iranian family law. The debate over “fourteen or 110 coins” does not by itself capture the issue: the economic value of *mahr* depends on a woman's access to divorce, income and housing, a share in property acquired during marriage, and effective enforcement of judgments.

The analysis treats *mahr* both as a legal financial right and, in the absence of alternative protections, as a limited source of bargaining leverage when a marriage breaks down. Its central conclusion is that structural reform is necessary, but weakening enforcement without creating substitute protections may shift more of the economic risk of divorce onto women.
