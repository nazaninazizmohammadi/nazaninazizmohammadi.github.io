## Summary

An increase in the number of women in political and administrative institutions does not, by itself, establish an increase in their decision-making authority. This essay examines the gap between women's numerical presence, the positions they hold, and the powers attached to those positions in Iran.

It considers how legal rules and their interpretation, appointment and promotion pathways, the gendered distribution of management portfolios, and informal networks of power restrict the conversion of women's qualifications and experience into political authority. It also distinguishes descriptive representation from substantive representation: the presence of women can contribute to policy change when they can influence agendas and decisions.

The essay proposes assessing participation through the level of responsibility and scope of authority, alongside transparent appointments, assessable selection criteria, and disaggregated data on senior positions.
