My research examines how public institutions distribute power, legal protection, and vulnerability. I focus on Iran while using comparative law, international human-rights standards, and empirical evidence to study the operation of legal rules in practice.

## Current research

### Evidence and access to justice in gender-based violence cases

I conduct source-verified research on evidentiary rules, judicial practice, and access to justice in sexual and gender-based violence cases in Iran. This work draws on legislation, judicial decisions, official legal opinions, and international human-rights standards.

### Gender and data-informed inequality research

At Virginia Tech's Dataism Lab, I study gender and socioeconomic disparities at the intersection of law, data, and global inequality. This work considers how empirical and quantitative methods can strengthen legal analysis without displacing institutional and contextual judgment.

### Constitutional power and regulatory governance

My public-law research addresses executive power, separation of powers, regulatory governance, constitutional implementation, and the relationship between courts, administrative institutions, and elected branches.
